Conditions

The part that depends on what happens

This is the only place you describe what has to happen — write it once, here, the way you'd say it out loud. The boxes below don't ask for it again; they just pin down which weeks it's measured over and which picks move each way.

Say it with a number

In December this gets settled against the actual scoring, so write it the way it will be counted. Name the player, name the pool, give a number.

  • say top 20 RB — a number and a pool, nothing to argue about
  • not an RB2 — half the league reads that as top 12, half as top 24
  • say top 2 RB — if you mean the second-best back, say the number
  • not the RB2 — reads as a tier to some and a rank to others; they're 22 spots apart
  • say 1,000 rushing yards, 1,500 yards from scrimmage, plays 14 games — all countable
  • not has a big year, stays healthy — nothing to measure
  • say top 20 flex, top 30 superflex — flex ranks RB/WR/TE together, superflex adds QBs; both work
Or hang it on a manager's season

A condition doesn't have to be about a player. How someone's team finishes settles just as cleanly — name the manager and say which finish you mean.

  • say Topper makes the playoffs, Maggie wins the championship, Topper gets a top 2 seed, she wins at least 9 games, he finishes dead last
  • also a surname is enough — unless two managers share one, in which case use a first name so it lands on the right team

If a condition doesn't name a number, it can't settle itself — it lands on the commissioner's desk in December, which is exactly the argument you were trying to avoid. Agree on the number now, while you're both still happy with the deal.
